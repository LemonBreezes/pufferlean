Trainer vs trainer, nothing else

RTX 5090 (sm_120, shared card, co-tenants idle) · Ryzen 9 9950X · both trainers, identical hyperparameters, envs stepping on CPU on BOTH sides · re-measured after the native-rollout escalation ladder (persistent buffer threads); supersedes the device-env-era report that previously lived at this address

What changed: the GPU-resident env capability (device breakout physics) was removed by product decision — it beat PufferLib by moving env stepping onto the GPU, a capability PufferLib deliberately omits, deployed on exactly one env. This page measures what remains: the training loop itself, like for like. Hyperparameter parity is structural (the config files are byte-identical to PufferLib's) plus an explicit horizon pin — earlier comparisons silently ran our horizon default (128) against their tuned 64. Since the first placement-matched report, a native-rollout redesign ladder (persistent buffer worker threads; see docs/native-rollout-design.md) landed a real, bit-identical win — these numbers reflect it.

Correctness: 22/22 parity, and what "bytewise" can mean

Two independently written implementations cannot produce byte-identical training runs — PufferLib draws from per-agent curand Philox streams and we use splitmix64, the tensor-core tilings differ, and bf16 rounding order differs. So parity is asserted where it is mathematically definable, through a transitive chain: our Lean f64 oracle is checked against the real PufferLib library, and every C and CUDA kernel we actually run is checked against that oracle.

All 39 built ocean environments were run through training; 29 show a clear learning signal and the rest are explained (sparse-reward at this budget, or zero-sum by construction). The sweep found three real defects, since fixed: a policy init that ignored fan-in and saturated the softmax at hidden 512 (maze went from a permanent flat zero to solving 79% of episodes), an adapter that never set double_pendulum's link geometry (every reward collapsed to exactly 0 through fmaxf(NaN,0)), and a missing map bank that left tower_climb with an unreachable goal. One divergence from PufferLib is deliberate and measured: our Gaussian logstd is state-dependent where theirs is a state-independent parameter — we built theirs and it trained worse on our stack, and on their own continuous env (squared_continuous) PufferLib reaches −0.76 at 6M steps and +0.29 at 200M, where we reach +0.66…+0.81 at 6M.

SPS conventions — and the full like-for-like table

Source forensics established that the two stacks' reported numbers measure different things. Their SPS (puf_log) is a windowed, post-warmup instantaneous rate: create_pufferl runs 11 eager epochs plus every CUDA-graph capture before their clock starts (0.18–0.45s, untimed), then resets all counters. Our number is total steps over total wall, including our own startup (0.02–0.20s). So all three conventions were measured on both stacks:

Steady-state for our side is a two-point fit over run lengths (squared's 20/100M and 100/200M pairs agree to 0.5%: 27.87M and 27.74M, startup 0.185–0.203s); theirs is their own windowed metric, cross-checked by the same fit. Every convention that counts all the work lands in our favour, and steady-state is a 1–2% deficit — inside the run-to-run band both stacks showed tonight (their squared varied 28.06–28.99M across runs). Forensics also established: backend = Serial in their squared.ini is a dead knob (parsed, never consumed); nothing in their rollout is env-conditional beyond the obs dtype; their per-step rollout GPU work (24µs per 2048-row graph) is matched by ours; and their train phase is measurably slower than ours (single-threaded build_cdf, index_copy at 10% of their GPU time).

Refuted on the way (built, measured, reverted)

Global-scratch fused Muon for medium/large models — squared −87%: one block serially grinding real GEMMs from global memory obliterates any enqueue saving. Launch fusion only pays when per-launch GPU work is trivial.
Single-GPU-thread launcher — disproven before building: buffer-count sweeps are flat, so the per-step sync cost is completion wait, not driver-mutex contention.
Rollout CUDA-graph replay — byte-identical, −0.7%: after the minibatch de-sync the host already runs ahead of the GPU; collapsing ~3072 launches had nothing left to win.
FR=32 WMMA tiling — −13%: the fused forward is scalar-stage-bound, not weight-load-bound, closing the whole prefetch/tiling family.
Spin-flag completion — re-refuted at small scale (−3%), and buffer-count / block-size sweeps — flat everywhere.
Env fan-out decoupled from forward width (M1) — flat-to-negative on all three envs at every ratio tested: the fused kernel does constant per-row work regardless of launch width.
Wide-GEMM forward arm (M6) — refuted before writing its budgeted ~350 LoC: the existing GEMM lineage, measured directly at width via an existing gate, loses to our own fused kernel by ~1.8× at h≤64, and going wider makes it worse (squared 16.7M→11.1M at true whole-batch width — losing buffer-level overlap costs more than any GEMM efficiency gained).
CPU training mode — a finding, not a refutation: NEITHER library has one. Ours is CUDA-only by design; their shipped trainer is the CUDA _C (their --cpu build lacks the trainer entry points).

Where the remaining gap lives

h ≤ 64 shapes (~0.77–0.78×) — diffuse per-step loop efficiency: their lean native C loop (one captured CUDA graph + one sync per step) vs our worker/sync/launch structure. A full escalation-ladder investigation (docs/native-rollout-design.md) found and landed the one real, low-risk win (persistent buffer threads); the rest of the gap resisted every incremental lever tried.
h128 shapes (0.61×) — their fused bf16 whole-net kernels still lead on GEMM-heavy widths; our tf32 WMMA fused forward narrows but does not match. Closing this further needs a genuinely new batched-across-buffers forward architecture (one big GEMM per stage, batched across all agents, launch-collapsed via captured graphs) — the same risk/scope class as a rejected all-native-driver design; flagged, not started.
What we keep — every path verified against the Lean f64 oracle: byte-identical trajectories wherever a change claims bit-identity, determinism + curve-tracking wherever a precision tier moves. The whole native-rollout ladder (5 rungs investigated) shipped without a single tolerance-class change.

Method. Sequential runs only (parallel GPU benches corrupt each other); co-resident Ollama/ComfyUI idle throughout; 40M env-steps per run. Hyperparameters: PufferLib's own tuned per-env configs (files byte-identical in both repos), horizon pinned to their effective value per env. Their numbers via a minimal probe driving pufferlib._C.create_pufferl/rollouts/train with their load_config; per-env _C rebuilds (needs a stub cuda_profiler_api.h, gcc-15, and a libomp5→libomp shim on this box); their original breakout build restored after. Learning verified on both sides every run (their pong solves at ep-return 21; ours saturates its normalized 1.0; squared curves track within the tf32 tolerance tier). Lineage.
